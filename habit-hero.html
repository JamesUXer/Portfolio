HabitHero: personalising goals to make progress understandable

A research-led redesign of onboarding, daily guidance and weekly review.

Individual academic project · UX researcher and designer · October 2025–April 2026

I owned discovery, journey design, prototyping and usability testing. Delivered: a refined Figma prototype; longer-term habit formation remains unproven.

Discover

I investigated what people needed from fitness tracking.

The brief asked for better progress tracking. I interviewed people about how they tracked activity and what helped or discouraged them.

Starting point

Help people track activity and understand their progress towards a goal.

I conducted five discovery interviews, then synthesised the findings into a persona, journey and design priorities.

Interviews showed that tracking could add pressure.

People described arbitrary targets, limited time and the pressure of tracking more information.

Excerpts from discovery interviews, reproduced in the discovery presentation script.

What the research changed

I concluded that progress feedback should explain what to do without adding pressure. I focused on helping people choose a realistic starting goal, understand daily progress and restart after missed days.

Research persona · synthesised from discovery

Ella, the time-poor parent

Making sense of conflicting information.

Key pain
Conflicting advice makes it hard to know what matters.
Key motivation
Clear guidance she can trust.

Define

I made goal setting the starting point for understanding progress.

A goal based on recent activity would give people a starting point for judging their progress. I used this as the reference point for daily status and weekly review.

What success would mean

Measure whether people accept the starter goal and complete setup, interpret their status correctly, and understand the weekly outcome. Longer-term evaluation would examine continued activity after goal adjustment.

Planned measures from the project’s goals–signals–metrics work, not achieved results.

What the design needed to do

  • Make the next action understandable.
  • Make completion clear.
  • Support restarting without shame.

I tested whether people understood the goals, feedback and controls.

Seven people tested goal setup, daily status, weekly rules and goal adjustment in moderated sessions.

Five of seven sessions exposed confusion about how daily status was calculated. Four highlighted insufficient notification control.

6 of 7 found the weekly criteria unclear.

The weekly rule allowed two missed days. The word “streak” suggested otherwise.

The response
State the five-day rule directly and explain that the days need not be consecutive.

The revision is documented. Improved comprehension after this change has not been established.

What people found useful

Goals that adapt to recent activity received positive responses in six of seven sessions. Notifications and quick check-ins were useful for recovery across all seven.

Deliver

I clarified the wording and controls, then delivered the final prototype.

I explained active times during onboarding, added notification controls and documented the final screens for handover.

Delivered Figma prototype · behavioural impact not yet validated.

Still unresolved

I tested comprehension and reactions to a prototype. These sessions did not establish improved retention or lasting exercise habits.

Permissions remained outstanding. Suitability of the adaptive goal logic still needs specialist validation. Retesting of the revised prototype has not been established. The next validation would check comprehension of the revised rules and controls before investigating longer-term behaviour.
